The Novice

My name is Stephanie Ijiogbe, self-proclaimed and true novice. I've been playing the guitar for about four years now, but yes, still a novice (I briefly considered renaming this site "Playing Guitar: the Art of Remaining a Novice"). I've always loved music, but for the longest time, I never entertained the idea of making/playing it; I was satisfied just listening to it.

I've learned how to play some rather basic songs like Amazing Grace and Twinkle, Twinkle, and you want to count Happy Birthday as one of them, be my guest. Though, you are I are going to be there for the better part of a n hour before I finish. And while I mostly play the guitar, I've also been experimenting with my sister's violin. As of now, I only have one guitar (acoutic), but if I get more serious about this hobby of mine, I might invest in another.

The Art of the Guitar

If you're unfamiliar with the instrument (you might think it strange but remember that not all of us have the same experiences), the guitar is a stringed instrument that makes sound by plicking or strumming the strings. It typically has about six strings, but can apparently go up to twelve (which is news to me). There are different types of guitars as well. From acoustic to electric to classical, each created their own way and offering their own unique sound .

Playing the guitar is a relatively popular hobby that allows musicians and average people alike to create and perform music. Some play for personal enjoyment, others play as a career, and the guitar is an instrument that can fit any style. Guitar playing typically involves techniques like strumming, fingerpicking, chord transitions, and slides, but beginners first learn basic chords and simple songs to get them started. But no matter the skill level, playing the guitar is an enriching and enjoyable enperience for all (admittedly maybe not so much for the people listening to you when you first get started).

Best Times to Play

My favorite time to play is near nighttime, when the sun is beginning to set. I find it's the most quiet and everybody is beginning to settle down. I've finished my tasks for the day and I don't have any obligations. Everyone has settled on an activity they're going to spend the night doing and usually don't have to worry about them needing me for it since they haven't asked me yet. Everything is perfect.

When it comes to seasons, I reserve my guitar playing for the Spring and Summer. Autumn and Winter are two seasons I don't like engaging with (my ancestors did not prepare me for weather like that). But Spring and Summer? Those seasons I can get behind. I especially love being huddled indoors while the clouds lose their minds outdoors, picking up the guitar by the window and just strumming whatever the hell I feel like (if you ever get the chance, try it; makes you feel like you're in a movie scene!). But the summer, with it clear, quiet nights are somehing else entirely, where it feels like the entire world has gone still and all you can hear are the strings of your guitar.

Best Locations to Play

I typically practice in my bedroom; it's where I'm most comfortable and I don't have to worry about being interrupted by anyone. And when you're a novice (like me), comfort is a really important thing, especially when you consider that everything else is about to get comfortable. My fingers really hated me when I first got started.

Sometimes though, when I'm home alone and the sun is beginning to set, I like to take my guitar to the backyard patio, under the gazebo for a little late-night strumming. I'm usually not trying to play any songs, but it's a lovely time to go over the chords I can remember and experiement on my own.

How To Get Started

My guitar actually came with tutotial book. It taught me a few chords and some easy popular songs as well like Twinkle, Twinkle and Happy Birthday. If you're able to purchase a deal like that, I recommend it. Since it'll be your first guitar, there really is no point in you getting one that absurdly expensive. It's the guitar you getting to know if it's something you'd like to continue with and the one you'll make many mistakes with, so don't get oee that'll make you refret the purchase or one you'll be too afraid to tweak.

I would also recommend youtube videos. I tried using apps, but it seems like the really good ones hide behind paywalls, and this was not a hobby I was ready to pay for. So I watched a bunch of youtube videos that taight more chords and melodies and others that teach you haw to play different songs. I would also advise to take it slow. Just becuase it's a song you like, it doesn't mean you should learn it. Some songs are harder to learn than others, so start easy and work your way up.

Why I Love the Guitar

I'm rather indecisive about everything that I do. It gets to the point where I overthink something and eventually just end up not doing anything. But one of the few things I didn't overthink was getting a guitar. It's why I love it so much. As one of the decisions I was able to go through with, it has a very special place in my heart.

I'm not very good and I didn't even buy it with my own money (if I had, then I'd definitely have to keep at it), but I keep coming back to it. And despite my slow learning, I'm still having a lot of fun. I still love to play, it hasn't gotten old yet and I doubt it will.
